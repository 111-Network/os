# Layer 7, lens 3: relay inside the 111 fabric (multi-hop, store-carry-forward, cross-hub delivery)

Research scout report, 2026-11-01, tree `3bf545b`. Code is cited as `file:line` under `/home/user/os`; `M/` = `5-messaging/crates/mesh111/src`. Web facts were fetched from raw.githubusercontent.com, PyPI, crates.io and the Go proxy on 2026-11-01 unless marked "(search snippet)" or "(unverified)". Airtime figures are my own Semtech time-on-air arithmetic (CRC on, CR 4/5, preamble 8, Meshtastic presets 16); they agree with `0-knowledge/concepts/lora-airtime-budget.md` within 3 %. Amendment labels A1 to A7 are the seven findings of `plan/layers/6-transport/research/00-code-seams.md` section 3 (A1 opaque per-peer connections for broadcast bearers, A2 transit forwarding, A3 fragment plan fixed at queue time, A4 shim codec and hop_limit, A5 INTRO is tcp-lan only, A6 no `remove_link`, A7 `Link::send` must not block).

## 1. Summary

- **RF multi-hop is already solved by an adopted program; what is missing is carry-forward across islands.** A stock MeshCore repeater floods our `GRP_DATA` frames blind, with its own seen-table and a 64-hop cap (D19 c; `0-knowledge/projects/meshcore.md`), so a device reaches a device through any number of repeaters with zero 111 forwarding. The gap is a recipient that is offline, or on another hub, or beyond an IP-only gap. That is hub custody, which is built for one hub only (`M/fabric.rs:2832-2833` answers `RejectUnknownDest` for any other dest).
- **Pick the minimal own forwarder (option 1): paired hubs, one hub-to-hub hop, transit only at hubs, cursor-based.** It needs exactly one mesh111 amendment on IP links (A2), no new process, no timer, and no routing protocol. Routing is the existing 8 B home-hub locator in the announce `appdata` (L2 A8) plus an owner-made hub pairing.
- **The A+ "summary vector" design does not fit what a hub can see.** A custodian sees `dest_hint`, size and counters, never the sender or stream (L5 section 9), so the section 4 form (epoch, prefix, ranges per sender/recipient stream) cannot be computed at a hub. A single-writer log per hub pair needs only a cursor (Syncthing's `{index ID, max sequence}`; the mailbox `MAILBOX_FETCH` cursor already does this). Set reconciliation over `pkt_ref` is only for multi-copy replication, which I put in v1.1+.
- **Reticulum as the router for sealed 111 frames is possible only through our LXMF helper**, with real costs: PLAIN and GROUP packets are dropped beyond 1 hop (`RNS/Transport.py:1642-1665`), so only SINGLE destinations and links are routed; a 160 B 111 packet becomes about 275 B on the wire (two RNode frames); a link costs 297 B in 3 packets. It adds A1, A3, A5, A6, A7 and a rule question (section 4). I rate it a v1.1 optional carrier, not the base.
- **A quantitative trap in the built code:** `CUSTODY_WRAP` is 120 B (`M/frag.rs:64`, pinned by a test at `:290`), not the "about 90 B" in L5 section 9. A member-to-hub `CUSTODY_PUT` therefore cannot be planned on the 160 B flood floor (`MtuTooSmall`) and leaves 24 B of text on a 233 B Meshtastic frame. Any hub-to-hub or member-to-hub custody over LoRa needs an unwrapped "hint custody" form first.
- **L2 section 13 answers:** announces are identity plus locator, never routing; Cat-1 relays are relay-blind (the stock repeater cannot verify, and 111 writes no relay firmware); no numeric announce rate limit of our own, because emission is event-driven (A6) and the bounds are airtime physics and capacity.

## 3. Findings that change the plan

1. **Stock repeaters give us RF flooding with their own loop control.** MeshCore floods with a seen-table and a hop-hash path appended per hop (`docs/packet_format.md:51-60`, max 64 path bytes); the KB records that a repeater floods group data without the channel key. Our shim `hop_limit` (3 bits, L5 section 3) is invisible to it, so A4 only matters at 111-aware transit nodes (hubs), never at Cat 1. Evidence for blind relay: `0-knowledge/projects/meshcore.md` (Layer 5 carriage facts).
2. **Reticulum drops multi-hop PLAIN and GROUP packets.** `RNS/Transport.py:1642-1665`: a PLAIN or GROUP packet with more than 1 hop is dropped and the receiving interface is charged a protocol violation. Only SINGLE destinations (paths from announces) and links route. So a Reticulum "broadcast" for the reserved `BROADCAST 0x30` can only ride an announce's `app_data`; by my arithmetic (MDU 464 minus the 148 B announce body) that is about 300 B, rate-limited by the 2 % cap and per-destination `announce_rate_target` (`interfaces.rst:1235-1245, 1398-1410`). (computed)
3. **Reticulum cost over LoRa, from the docs and my arithmetic.** Link setup is 3 packets, 297 B (`understanding.rst:355`: 83 + 115 + 99); path request 51 B; announce 167 B; announces capped at 2 % per interface and forwarded to at most 128 hops (`understanding.rst:246-259`, `Transport.py:118`); paths last 1 week, 1 day on access-point interfaces, 6 h on roaming (`Transport.py:126-128`); path requests are throttled to one per 20 s (`:138`). On RNode SF8/125 kHz the link setup alone is 0.92 s of airtime before any hop repeats it; the 2 % cap allows about 149 announces per hour per interface (72 s at 0.48 s each). A 160 B 111 packet in a single-destination Reticulum packet is about 275 B (19 B header, 32 B ephemeral key, 16 B IV, padding, 32 B HMAC) and RNode splits above 254 B (`lora-airtime-budget.md`), costing 0.80 s versus 0.46 s raw. Packet MDU is 464 plain and 383 encrypted (`Packet.py:101-110`). (the 275 B figure is computed, not fetched)
4. **What an external process can use without importing RNS.** Observation: `rnstatus -j` with `-A` announce stats, `-P` path-request stats, `-l` link stats and `--attach/--detach/--reload` (`using.rst:339-366`); `rnpath -t -j` path table with hops and `-r` rates (`using.rst:503-535`). Carriage: `rncp` as a spool carrier (`using.rst:614-700`), and the LXMF helper (D26 #4, `6-transport/README.md:325-334`), whose `send {fields}` already carries a structured custom field. Nothing else: the shared instance speaks the Reticulum wire protocol, so a non-Python client is a reimplementation (rule 1).
5. **Upstream disowns Rust reimplementations.** The Reticulum README (lines 84-121) calls the Python code "the reference implementation", lists only microReticulum (C++) and Reticulum-Go as acceptable, and says it "strongly advises against" any other. Reticulum-rs (MIT, 0.1.0, 2025-10-14) and Leviculum (AGPL) are unlisted. Confirms `LICENSING.md` section 1 and parked note P5: no Rust Reticulum in 111d.
6. **`CUSTODY_WRAP = 120 B`, not about 90 B** (`M/frag.rs:60-64`; test `:290`). `plan_mtu` subtracts it for `Route::Hub` (`M/fabric.rs:1855`). At 160 B: room 39 B, below the 88 B minimum sealed packet (64 outer + 8 inner + 16 `pair_mac`), so `MtuTooSmall`. At 233 B: 112 B item, 24 B of text. Custody-wrapped sends are viable on `tcp-lan` and on Reticulum links (MDU 383 leaves 262 B of item, about 174 B of text), not on flood floors.
7. **The hub cannot build section 4 summary vectors.** L5 section 3 shows the visible header fields are `counter` (per-peer, interleaved across senders for one dest), `dest_hint`, `prio_flags`; `sender_ref`, `msg_seq`, stream and kind are inside the seal. A hub's only sequence is its own `custody_seq` (`M/fabric.rs:2850`), and its only content id is `pkt_ref` (`M/custody.rs:105`). Hence: forward direction is a cursor per hub pair; multi-copy is set reconciliation over 16 B `pkt_ref`, not a prefix-and-ranges vector.
8. **Built dedup layers.** (a) Hub: `custody_put` dedups on `pkt_ref` and answers `DUPLICATE` (`M/fabric.rs:2837-2840`). (b) Receiver: never redelivers below the contiguous prefix, up to 4 epochs and 8 ranges (L5 section 4). (c) Relay flood ring `epk[0..8]` in 256 entries is specified (L5 section 4, line 230) and the key exists (`M/envelope.rs:473`), but no ring is wired. A hub that transits needs (c) only on a broadcast bearer; stock repeaters carry their own.
9. **The schema already names transit.** `Dir::Transit` is documented "Transit custody held for members (hub role)" (`M/store/rows.rs:49, 137`), but nothing writes it (seams A2). The state ladder survives intact: a hub-to-hub `CUSTODY_ACK ACCEPTED` from B is still "a mailbox node saved it" (`relayed`), and only the recipient's `ACK` gives `delivered` (`store-and-forward-states.md`).
10. **Routing hint is already on the wire.** Contacts carry an 8 B truncated `home_hub` in `appdata` (L5 section 7.3); `route()` uses it only to explain a queued send (`M/fabric.rs:1820-1850`, `hinted_elsewhere`). That is enough to pick the destination's hub among paired hubs; no routing protocol is needed.
11. **LXMF propagation is real anti-entropy, with a price.** Peers exchange offers of transient ids and fetch what they lack; senders and peerings pay proof-of-work stamps (`LXMPeer.py:227-262`, peering cost 18 and stamp cost 16 defaults per the KB). The stamp cost is upstream policy, not ours; on a Pi or phone it is real CPU.

## 4. Recommendation

### 4.1 Options

| | Option | Moving parts | Amendments to mesh111 (A1 to A7) | Cat-1 relay does | Resident weight added |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Hub star plus paired hubs, one hub-to-hub hop** (own forwarder, minimal) | owner-made hub pairing (the section 7.3 code flow, mutual); `on_custody_put` accepts custody for a dest whose `home_hub` hint matches a paired hub and stores `Dir::Transit`; a forwarder pumps Transit rows to the paired hub as `CUSTODY_PUT`, evicts on its `ACCEPTED`/`DUPLICATE`; cursor per hub pair on reconnect | **A2 only** on IP links. On LoRa add A1, A3, A6, A7, A5 (the L6 second-release set, D25 F6) plus an unwrapped hint-custody form (finding 6). **A4 not needed** | MeshCore flood, blind, for device-to-device and device-to-hub RF | none (event-driven on link and contact events) |
| **2** | **Reticulum as router via the LXMF helper (`rns-bridge`)** | helper gains a "111 frame" mode: the sealed packet as an LXMF field or a raw destination; rnsd `enable_transport`; propagation nodes carry offline recipients | A1 (LXMF dest hash as `PeerAddr::Opaque`), A3 (route-specific MTU 287 vs 160), A5 (first contact: dest hash must ride the card or `appdata` TLV), A6, A7 (JSON-lines pipe is already a queue). **Not A2, not A4** | nothing: stock MeshCore repeaters do not carry Reticulum; a Reticulum Cat-1 needs microReticulum_Firmware, a second fleet | helper plus `lxmd` when on; both already optional |
| **3** | **Epidemic anti-entropy between hubs (A+ as specified, multi-copy)** | option 1 plus replicas on several hubs, evict-first, `pkt_ref` set reconciliation (Negentropy shape) or EBT-style gossip, the relay-flood ring | A2, plus the ring (finding 8c) and new control kinds | as option 1 | memory for id sets |
| **4** | **IP-level mesh under `tcp-lan`** (babeld, Yggdrasil) | none of ours | **none**; `tcp-lan` is IP-agnostic | nothing | a daemon, root or a TUN |

Option 4 is not Layer 7: `tcp-lan` already reaches any IP address, so a Wi-Fi mesh with babeld on Linux hubs makes hubs reachable with no 111 change. It belongs, if at all, in the Layer 6 spine as an optional component recipe (Cat 3/4 Linux only: route changes need root, and Termux has neither root nor a TUN). I say "out of scope" for Layer 7.

### 4.2 My pick and the version split

- **v1: option 1 on IP links only** (LAN, VPN or whatever IP path the gateway lens delivers). One amendment (A2) in a locked crate, owner-visible: replace the `RejectUnknownDest` branch at `M/fabric.rs:2832-2833` with "paired-hub forward", add a hub-peer table, and bound the loop by construction: a hub never forwards an item it received from a paired hub, so a star of paired hubs has exactly one inter-hub hop and no cycles. Chains of three or more hubs wait for v1.1.
- **v1.1: (a) the radio form of option 1** (hint custody without the 120 B wrap: a member floods the sealed packet, the hub holds it by `dest_hint` for its enrolled members and answers a sealed `CUSTODY_ACK`; this is what L5 section 3 means by "locator for relays and mailboxes"), with A1, A3, A6, A7, A5; **(b) option 2 as an optional long-haul carrier**, only after the owner answers the rule question below; **(c) option 3's multi-copy** if three or more hubs need redundancy.
- **Why not option 2 as the base:** it doubles LoRa airtime for small frames (finding 3), it puts a resident Python helper plus an rnsd transport role on the path of every cross-hub message (against the Pi floor), it forks the relay fleet (MeshCore for 111 frames, RNode/Reticulum for this), and it leaves "delivery" to a library whose own states stop at `SENT` for propagated sends (`lxmf.md`). It does give true multi-hop across IP-less islands with an adopted router, which option 1 never will; that is the honest trade.

### 4.3 Airtime arithmetic for hub-to-hub frames (computed; one frame is the unwrapped 161 B sealed packet)

| Link | 161 B data frame | cursor or ACK frame (94 B) | 3-frame summary (A+ 20 dests) | custody-wrapped 233 B | 161 B frames per hour at 1 % duty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MeshCore narrow SF7/62.5 kHz | 0.52 s | 0.33 s | 1.57 s | 0.74 s | 68 |
| RNode SF8/125 kHz | 0.46 s | 0.29 s | 1.38 s | 0.65 s | 78 |
| Meshtastic MediumFast | 0.43 s | 0.28 s | 1.28 s | 0.59 s | 84 |
| Meshtastic LongFast | 1.46 s | 0.97 s | 4.38 s | 1.99 s | 24 |

A+ summary vectors at about 9 B per destination (hint 4, epoch 2, prefix 2, range count 1) and 71 B of payload per 160 B frame mean 7 entries per frame, so 20 members cost 3 frames per direction per sync (about 3 s each way on the narrow preset, 8.8 s on LongFast, where a 1 % band allows 36 s of transmission per hour in total). A cursor sync is one frame each way. Flooding multiplies channel time: each stock repeater in range repeats once, so one message plus its ACK across 5 repeaters on the narrow preset is about (0.52 + 0.33) x 6 = 5.1 s of channel time. These are physics, not limits of ours; the adapter advertises them (`PathProfile.airtime`, L5 section 8).

### 4.4 Loop and duplicate suppression, option 1

Layer by layer: (1) RF: the repeater's seen-table and 64-hop cap; (2) hub: `pkt_ref` dedup returns `DUPLICATE` and the forwarder treats it as done; (3) structural: one inter-hub hop, never forward what came from a paired hub; (4) end: the receiver's prefix and range state never redelivers. No hop counter is needed on `tcp-lan` and none exists in the sealed packet; the shim `hop_limit` stays unbuilt (A4) until a 111 transit node floods on a broadcast bearer.

### 4.5 The L2 section 13 hand-offs

- **Announce-as-routing vs identity-only: identity-only plus locator.** The announce stays a self-authenticating key card (`137 to 169 B`) whose `appdata` TLV (Layer 7 appends after v1, A8) carries the 8 B home-hub id; Layer 7 may add a role TLV. Reticulum's announce-as-routing is what makes its 2 % cap and its flood; our star needs "which hub", not "which path". Revisit only if option 2 ships, where the LXMF destination hash is the extra locator.
- **Cat-1 verification: relay-blind.** A stock repeater cannot verify an Ed25519 announce it cannot parse, 111 writes no relay firmware (D19 c), and verify-before-relay would hand an attacker a CPU lever: the KB records 1.5 to 2 s per Ed25519 verify on a Cortex-M4 class part (`meshcore.md`). Defences sit at the endpoints: strict verification, immutable pinned keys, the bounded 256-entry pin store (L2 section 6.3). Residual stated: junk floods cost shared airtime; that is MeshCore's scope control (region DENY_FLOOD), not ours.
- **Announce rate limit: no number of ours.** Emission is on demand only (A6), so there is no periodic rate to cap; receivers are bounded by capacity (pin store 256, pending requests 256, L5 section 7.2). For Reticulum the 2 % cap lives in rnsd per interface (`announce_cap`) and we leave it at the default.

### 4.6 Rule 1 and Rule 3 statements

- Option 1 imports nothing from Reticulum. Option 2 needs the **already-named helper** to gain a frame mode, which is "import RNS" inside an existing program but a new use; if the owner prefers a separate program it is a **new importing program of ours and a rule question, never the default**. The no-import variants are the LXMF carrier with the sealed packet in a custom field (no new helper capability beyond what `send {fields}` has) and the `rncp` spool.
- Option 1 adds no process, listener, timer or open file to the required configuration. The hub role already exists on Cat 4 and opt-in nodes. Recording RSS is not needed for option 1; for option 2 measure helper plus rnsd RSS under a forwarding load and record it in `7-relay/TASKS.md`.
